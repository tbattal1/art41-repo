# ART-41 — Case coding sheet: PIRAEUS (#6)
**Coding date:** 2026-07-08 (Batch 2) | **Codebook:** v2.2 at coding; reconciled under v2.6 (K1–K16) on 2026-11-03 | **Status:** locked | **Window:** governance as of end-2019
**Governance pattern:** Privatized port authority under a single foreign state-owned operator, vertically integrated (COSCO controls both the landlord PPA and the dominant container operator PCT).

## Structural summary
- PPA/OLP (Piraeus Port Authority S.A.) = port authority, holding a 2002 concession from the Greek state (40 → 50 years, to 2052).
- 2008–09: COSCO took over Pier II + III under a 35-year concession (PCT — Piraeus Container Terminal).
- **2016: COSCO acquired 67 % of PPA (HRADF privatization).** → COSCO is both majority PPA shareholder and container operator. The Greek state retains 7 % + a seat on the board.
- Hence in 2019: PPA (COSCO-controlled) as landlord + COSCO (PCT) as dominant operator = **vertically integrated foreign-state structure.**
- PPA is listed on the Athens Stock Exchange.

## C1 — Regulatory autonomy → **0.70**
- **Subject rule (K9):** PPA S.A. is the landlord and grants concessions → subject = PPA. Since 2016 a COSCO-controlled private company (listed, Athens SE).
- **Status:** Privatized joint-stock company; broad commercial autonomy within the concession agreement (including the right to grant concessions to third parties). Greek state is a minority shareholder (7 %) with 1 board seat → limited state tutelage.
- **Control type:** Private company with high commercial autonomy; the state is a minority shareholder (the reverse of Rotterdam's public-shareholder logic — here the state is the MINORITY). No independent-regulator ceiling.
- **Score: 0.70** — privatized and commercially autonomous, but bound to the state framework of the concession agreement + the state's board seat. (Not 0.95, because operations are tied to the strict terms of the 2052 concession agreement and a mandatory investment schedule.)
- Coder's note: the alternative of 0.95 was considered in the working file and rejected as too high given the strict concession terms; 0.70 confirmed as final (author decision, 2026-07-08; analysed matrix).

## C2 — Ownership hybridity → **0.30** (LOW — single integrated operator)
- Container: almost entirely COSCO/PCT (Pier II + III) + Pier I operated by PPA (itself COSCO-controlled).
- Single dominant operator AND that operator is also the owner of the landlord → vertically integrated, NO plurality.
- Resembles Durban (single integrated operator) but with a specific feature: **hybridity is low because a single foreign-state actor is both landlord and operator.**
- **Score: 0.30** (private operation exists, but monopoly + vertical integration → low hybridity; Durban 0.30 precedent).

## C4 — Compound lock-in → **0.44** (K12)
- **(a) Regulatory rigidity (2005–2019):** The 2016 privatization is a major reform (state → private); afterwards the concession structure is fixed. ≥1 major reform within the window → 0.33.
- **(b) Financial lock-in (Branch A):** **Long concession — to 2052 (33 years remaining in 2019) → 25–40 band → 0.67.** In addition: COSCO's mandatory investment schedule + the 35-year PCT concession. Strong lock-in.
- **(c) Social legitimacy gap (K12, operational):** **The Piraeus dockworkers' union struck REPEATEDLY** (2014–16, anti-privatization, affecting throughput); at the edge of the window, but the 2015–16 strikes were operationally effective. At least 1 cluster → 0.33. (Operational strike cluster within 2015–2019: anti-privatization strikes.)
- **Composite:** (0.33 + 0.67 + 0.33)/3 = 1.33/3 = **0.44**.
- Coder's note: the working file's C4 heading read 0.55 while the body computed 0.44 and carried its own correction line ("not 0.55 — corrected"); heading/body inconsistency in the working file resolved in favour of the computed value, 0.44 (analysed matrix). The inclusion of the 2015–16 strike cluster in C4c (0.33) was confirmed (author decision, 2026-07-08).
