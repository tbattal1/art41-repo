## Structural summary
- **Landlord + regulator:** Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA) — statutory board (2 February 1996), under MOT purview. An academic source describes MPA directly as "the landlord and port regulator" (Teo & Bhattacherjee-type source / ScienceDirect 2000).
- **Operator:** PSA (corporatized 1 October 1997; Temasek sole shareholder). Container operator; not the landlord, does not lease to third parties → K9 single-operator branch → subject = MPA.
- **Second operator:** Jurong Port (bulk/general cargo; Temasek/state-linked).

## C1 — Regulatory autonomy → **0.50**
- **Subject rule (K9):** MPA (landlord-regulator). PSA is the operator, so its freedom of action is recorded under C2.
- **Evidence:** MPA is a statutory board with its own legal personality and its own budget (self-funding from port dues); tariff setting and licensing are autonomous. However, major strategic investment (Tuas Port multi-phase national reclamation; reclamation is in practice an MPA + government decision) sits at national/Cabinet level; MPA is under MOT purview.
- **Band match:** "Autonomous legal entity exists, but strategic decisions subject to central approval" → **0.50**.
- **Control typology:** (i) partial hierarchical tutelage (MOT purview + national strategic investment) → lowers autonomy; but operational/tariff autonomy is preserved → not 0.05/0.30, but 0.50.
- **Sources:** MPA Act 1996 (No. 7 of 1996); NLB Singapore; MPA.gov.sg (Tuas phases); ScienceDirect (2000) "MPA... is the landlord and port regulator".

## C2 — Ownership hybridity → **0.30**
- **Metric:** ~100 % of container capacity operated by PSA; PSA is corporatized but 100 % Temasek (state) owned. Single dominant (in practice sole) container operator.
- **K13:** Corporatization ≠ privatization; Temasek ownership is state capital → does not count as "private". The COSCO/DP World note concerns *foreign* operators, not a national state operator.
- **Band match:** "<20 % private participation (public-dominant in containers... formal landlord structure)" → **0.30**. Durban precedent (corporatized public-dominant → 0.30).
- **Sources:** Temasek (sole shareholder); PSA/MPA corporate sources.

## C3 — Multi-level coordination density → **0.50** (2/4)
1. **City–port body = 0.** City-state; no separate municipal/city–port coordination body — integration is intra-state hierarchy (symmetry with the C3.2 v2 vertical-hierarchy exclusion).
2. **Active participation in a national coordination body = 1.** Sea Transport Industry Transformation Map (ITM; launched 2018; MPA + industry + unions + other government agencies; five tripartite MOUs at launch). Horizontal multi-stakeholder national coordination framework, active within the window.
3. **International network active = 1.** Nine bilateral Green & Digital Shipping Corridors (including Rotterdam, LA/Long Beach, Tianjin, Japan, Australia, Shandong); IMO leadership; IAPH.
4. **Port-specific multi-actor crisis protocol (beyond the legal minimum) = 0.** No FERM-equivalent body found as of 2019; ISPS is universal (K11 → not counted). The ITM "Resilience" pillar was added only in the 2022 refresh.
- **Sources:** MPA media (Sea Transport ITM 2018); MOT green transport (GDSC list).

## C4 — Compound lock-in → **0.33**
- **(a) Regulatory rigidity = 1.** No structural *governance* reform in the 2005–2019 window. Corporatization (1997) and the PSA International holding (2003) pre-date the window. In-window activities (Tuas consolidation 2012/2015, digitalPORT@SG 2019, ITM 2018, PPT Phases 3 & 4) are infrastructure/digital/programmatic — the governance model (MPA regulator + PSA operator + Temasek owner) did not change. → 0 reforms → 1.
- **(b) Financial lock-in = 0.** Branch B (state-operator port): PSA/Temasek face zero financial constraint — very high profitability, billions in Tuas capex, most advanced automation. 0/3 indicators.
- **(c) Social legitimacy gap = 0.** No operationally effective dispute cluster in 2015–2019 (no strike/blockade/throughput-stopping event; tripartite industrial peace). 0 clusters.
- **Mean:** mean(1, 0, 0) = **0.33**.
- **Analytical note (Discussion):** High C4a + zero C4b/C4c = "stability-because-optimized" ≠ "lock-in-because-blocked" (Mersin/Durban). Lock-in ↔ dysfunction decoupling; the symmetric counterpart of Durban's C5/C4c decoupling.

## C5 — Social-value integration → **0.40** (1/3)
1. **Binding environmental/community mandate (in founding statute) = 1.** The MPA Act obliges MPA to "ensur[e] a clean marine environment" — statutory public-duty language.
2. **Stakeholder representation body in governance = 0.** No statutory PCC-type stakeholder body; the ITM is already counted under C3.2 (double counting avoided).
3. **Social-green conditions in concession/licence terms = 0.** The Maritime Singapore Green Initiative (2011–) consists of voluntary incentive programs (Note #8 → voluntary does NOT count). Emission targets (60 % reduction by 2030 vs 2005) are commitments, not binding licence conditions.
- **Sources:** MPA Act 1996; MPA, "Maritime Singapore Green Initiative" (Sustainability page): "These are voluntary programmes designed to recognise and provide incentives" — Green Ship, Green Port Infrastructure, Green Craft, Green Energy and Technology and Green Awareness Programmes; S$100 million pledged over 5 years in 2011, enhanced 2013/2019/2022 (https://www.mpa.gov.sg/maritime-singapore/sustainability, accessed 2026-11-03); MOT [previously: Sustainable-ships].
