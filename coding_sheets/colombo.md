---

## Structural summary
- **Landlord + regulator + partial operator:** Sri Lanka Ports Authority (SLPA) — state statutory authority under the SLPA Act No. 51/1979; owns all ports, operates JCT directly, leases to SAGT/CICT.
- **Operators:** JCT (SLPA/state, 100 %), SAGT (John Keells consortium, 1999, SLPA 15 % minority), CICT (China Merchants 85 % + SLPA 15 %, 35-year BOT, operational 2013).
- **Concessions:** ≤35 years, leasehold; SLPA holds a minority (15 %) on behalf of the state. Cabinet approval mandatory. Geopolitics: China (CICT) vs India/Japan (ECT MoU 2019, collapsed under a union veto).

---

## C1 — Regulatory autonomy → **0.30**
- **Subject (K9):** SLPA (landlord; leases to SAGT/CICT → second branch).
- **Evidence:** Broad statutory powers ("wide and far-reaching powers" under the statute), appoints its own harbourmaster, operational autonomy at JCT. BUT concessions are Cabinet-approved ("SLPA will necessarily seek cabinet approval"); deep politicization + geopolitical steering (2009 CICT single bid, unsolicited proposals) + union veto determined operational decisions (ECT).
- **Tier:** Legal personality + broad powers, but strategic and operational decisions sit within the national political system → **0.30**. (0.50 would be defensible; the depth of politicization pulled the score to 0.30.)
- **Sources:** SLPA Act 1979 (slpa.lk); Lexology Ports & Terminals SL 2019 (Cabinet approval, JCT wholly SLPA).

## C2 — Ownership hybridity → **0.70**
- **Metric:** Container capacity ~64 % privately operated (CICT ~2.4M design capacity per SLPA, China Merchants; SAGT ~2M, John Keells) + state JCT ~36 %; ≥2 independent private operators. SLPA holds 15 % minority stakes in SAGT/CICT.
- **Calibration decision (author decision, case-by-case, 08.07.2026):** The scale has no clean "60–80 % multi-operator" tier; 0.95 (>80 %) is too high, 0.50 (~40–60 % mixed) too low. Majority-private + ≥2 independent operators BUT a meaningful state JCT (~35 %) → **0.70** (case-by-case judgement; no new tier added).
- **Note:** China Merchants in CICT/SAGT counts as "private" (state-capitalized foreign operator, per the codebook note); the SLPA 15 % minority is not operational control.
- **Sources:** Sri Lanka Ports Authority presentation "Port of Colombo" (data to April 2019; three terminals JCT, SAGT, CICT; CICT "China Merchant 85% + SLPA 15%", "PPP / BOT", "Annual Capacity – 2.4m TEU'S", "Fully Operational"; https://cimc.lk/images/speaker_presenationss/0915/Port_of_Colombo.pdf, accessed 2026-11-03); SLPA news portal, CICT section ("CMPH holds 85% of the partnership whilst the balance 15% is being held by SLPA"; "35 year Build Operate and Transfer Agreement with SLPA"; https://news.slpa.lk/index.php/category/terminals/cict/page/2, accessed 2026-11-03); John Keells Holdings PLC Annual Report 2019/20, p. 23 ("42 per cent stake in SAGT – container terminal (capacity of ~2 million TEUs)"; SAGT volume 2,066,192 TEU in 2019/20; https://keells.lk/resource/reports/annual-reports/John_Keells_Holdings_PLC_AR_2019_20_CSE.pdf, accessed 2026-11-03); SAGT "About us" (backed by John Keells Holdings, APM Terminals, Sri Lanka Ports Authority and Peony Investments/Evergreen; "commenced operations at Queen Elizabeth Quay in 1999" as Sri Lanka's first PPP container terminal; https://www.sagt.com.lk/about-us.html, accessed 2026-11-03); Lexology (SLPA 15 % SPV minority; JCT wholly SLPA) [previously: Wikipedia/UNIS].
