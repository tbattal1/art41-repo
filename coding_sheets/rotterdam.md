# ART-41 — Case coding sheet: ROTTERDAM (#1)
**Coding date:** 07.07.2026 (pilot sheet and verification addendum, same day) | **Codebook:** v2 (pilot; cells re-checked against v2.1–v2.6, K1–K16) | **Status:** locked | **Window:** governance as of end-2019 (pre-shock state)
**Coder:** author, with AI coding assistance; author sign-off
**Governance pattern:** corporatized landlord port authority (Havenbedrijf Rotterdam N.V.) with two public shareholders; fully private multi-operator container handling; low compound lock-in.

## Structural summary
- Havenbedrijf Rotterdam N.V. — converted in 2004 from a municipal department into a corporatized company; unlisted public limited company.
- Two public shareholders: Municipality of Rotterdam ~70 %, Dutch State ~30 %. Two formal general meetings per year plus informal quarterly meetings; major decisions reserved to the general meeting.
- Landlord model: the authority does not operate container terminals; revenue model = leases + port dues.
- Container handling (2019): at least three independent groups — ECT Delta + Euromax (Hutchison Ports), APM Terminals (2 terminals, Maersk group), RWG (DP World 30 % + CMA CGM 10 % + three carriers at 20 % each).
- Post-2019 structural change (outside the coding window): in 2024 Hutchison sold ECT to TiL + IFM, and DP World terminated its Amaliahaven concession (November 2024). No effect on the scores; to be noted as "ownership turbulence during the shock" in the Ş2 outcome interpretation.

## C1 — Regulatory autonomy → **0.95**

**Subject rule applied (K1/K9, Not#5+#12):** the actor actually exercising the landlord function is Havenbedrijf Rotterdam N.V., which leases to third-party operators → the N.V. is the C1 subject.
**Control typology (K10, Not#1+#13):** control operates through type (ii) — general-meeting rights of public shareholders — not through hierarchical ministerial tutelage (type i). Type (ii) does not reduce autonomy.

## C4 — Compound lock-in → **0.33**

**(a) Regulatory rigidity (window 2005–2019) → 0.33**
One significant reform: the 2004 corporatization, which lies exactly at the boundary of the 15-year window used in the pilot (2004–2019); the 2014 update of the articles of association is secondary regulation. No record of a failed reform attempt. → 1 reform → 0.33.
**Coder's note (codebook revision note #2, resolved as Not#2 in v2):** the "last 15 years" window produced a boundary case (the 2004 reform fell exactly on the edge of a 2019 coding). Codebook v2 rule: a reform whose year equals the window's start year is INCLUDED; and the window is applied as a FIXED calendar window 2005–2019 to all cases (cross-case comparability). Under the fixed window the 2004 corporatization is the one reform counted for Rotterdam; C4a = 0.33.

**(b) Financial lock-in → 0.33** (Branch A — concessioned/leased port; capacity-weighted remaining concession term as of 2019)

**Non-definite-number warning (mis-citation prevention):** Maersk's "max 25y amortization", "50y land lease" and "20y concession-right amortization" figures are ACCOUNTING ceilings, not contract terms. The "25-year cooperation" in 2025 press coverage refers to APMT's presence in Rotterdam since 2000, not to the MV2 concession. None of these enter C4b.

**Composite effect:** both terminals are in the 0.33 band → whatever the capacity weighting, **Rotterdam C4b = 0.33.**

**Manuscript footnote (ready text):** *"The RWG concession term (25 years, MOL 2007) is documented; the APMT MV2 term is not publicly disclosed and is inferred to fall in the same band based on the concurrent award context and Maersk's 25-year infrastructure amortisation ceiling. Both interpretations place Rotterdam's financial lock-in in the 10–25 year band."*

**Documented uncertainty:** the APMT MV2 contract term is not publicly disclosed; it is coded 0.33 by inference (same band under every plausible reading), with the inference stated transparently. The maturity of ECT's older Delta land leases was also not sourced; it cannot move C4b out of the 0.33 band because both MV2 terminals and any plausible ECT reading fall in the 10–25 band or below.
**Coder's note:** the pilot sheet recorded C4b as MISSING-pending (no estimate under the anti-DIPAN rule; action item: PoR Annual Report 2019 concession-maturity footnotes + RWG/APMT press archive). **(c) Social-legitimacy gap (window 2015–2019) → 0.33**
One major documented dispute cluster in 2015–2019: the 24-hour strike of January 2016 against full automation — the first strike in 13 years, ~3,000 participants, ITF/ETF-supported; resolved in July 2016 with a job-security agreement (FNV Havens had claimed 800 jobs were at risk). This is an operationally effective acute dispute (K12: strike), so it counts. Single cluster = 0.33.
Sources: Equal Times (11.01.2016), Offshore Energy (12/2015, 01/2016), Vice/Motherboard, JOC (06.07.2016).
**Coder's note (codebook revision note #3, resolved as Not#3 in v2):** the "number of events" rule created ambiguity — were the December 2015 ultimatum + January 2016 strike + subsequent threats ONE dispute cluster or three events? Codebook v2 rule: events tied to the same dispute subject count as ONE CLUSTER (cluster definition: same parties + same demand + <12 months apart). Applied here: one cluster → 0.33.

**C4 composite:** (0.33 + 0.33 + 0.33)/3 = **0.33** — all three sub-dimensions in the lower band; a "low compound lock-in" profile.
