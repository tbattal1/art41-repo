# ART-41 — Case coding sheet: MELBOURNE (#22)
**Coding date:** 07.07.2026 (pilot sheet and verification addendum, same day) | **Codebook:** v2 (pilot; cells re-checked against v2.1–v2.6, K1–K16) | **Status:** locked | **Window:** governance as of end-2019 (pre-shock state)
**Coder:** author, with AI coding assistance; author sign-off
**Pilot role:** stress test of the financialization end of the configuration space.
**Governance pattern:** privatized landlord — the landlord function itself is held by a private lessee (Port of Melbourne, PoM; 50-year lease from 2016) that leases berths/land to third-party stevedores; maritime/safety functions retained by the state (Victorian Ports Corporation Melbourne, VPCM); independent price regulation by the Essential Services Commission (ESC).

## Structural summary
- Landlord function privatized: PoM (private lessee) is the management body that leases berths/land to third-party operators. Retained by the state: maritime/safety functions (Victorian Ports Corporation Melbourne) + price regulation (ESC).
- 50-year lease, 1 November 2016 → 2066; consideration AUD 9.7 billion; lessee: Future Fund (Australian sovereign wealth fund) + QIC + GIP + OMERS (Canadian pension fund) — an institutional-investor consortium.
- Statutory Pricing Order: tariffs may rise once a year by at most CPI (Tariff Adjustment Limit); ESC annual compliance statement + 5-yearly review; export price decision (container wharfage −2.5 %/year to 2019-20). PoM's own description: "the most heavily regulated port in the country".
- Three independent stevedores (2019): DP World Melbourne, Patrick, VICT (ICTSI — operating at Webb Dock East since December 2016, "the port's third stevedore").
- Statutory annual port licence fee ~AUD 80M (CPI-indexed).
- Outside the window (Ş2): the ESC's first 5-yearly review (2016–2021) found "significant and sustained non-compliance" — revenue requirement overstated by AUD 300–650M.

## C1 — Regulatory autonomy → **0.70**

**Subject rule applied (K9, Not#12 — generated by this case):** the C1 subject is the actor that actually exercises the landlord/management function, PUBLIC OR PRIVATE. Distinction test: does the actor lease/license to third-party operators? In Mersin MIP is a single-operator concessionaire (does not lease to third parties) → subject = TCDD (0.05). In Melbourne PoM is the landlord → subject = PoM. Consistent and defensible; approved by the author (K9).
**Control typology (K10, Not#13 — generated by this case):** three control types: (i) hierarchical tutelage (ministerial approval) — lowers autonomy; (ii) public shareholder's general-meeting rights (Rotterdam) — does not lower it; (iii) independent-regulator ceiling (Melbourne) — only the AFFECTED decision area is counted as non-autonomous. The three-way typology entered v2; conceptually valuable in the Discussion as well ("autonomy — independence from whom?").

## C4 — Compound lock-in → **0.44** (near-pure financial lock-in profile)

**(a) Regulatory rigidity (window 2005–2019) → 0**
Two significant reforms in the window: (1) the 2016 Delivering Victorian Infrastructure (Port of Melbourne Lease Transaction) Act + the Pricing Order regime; (2) the **Port Management Further Amendment Act 2012**, which established the SEMP regime (safety–environment licensing regime + ministerial-guidelines framework) — identified in SEMP §1. No record of a failed attempt. Rule: ≥2 reforms = 0.
**Coder's note:** the pilot sheet scored 0.33 (one reform) with the open question whether the 2012 port licence fee arrangement counted as a second reform. The verification round resolved it against the lock-in reading: the 2012 Act is a second significant regulatory reform → C4a 0.33 → 0.

**(b) Financial lock-in (Branch A) → 1.0** — financialization end confirmed
50-year lease, 1 November 2016 → 2066; remaining term in 2019 = **47 years** → >40 band = 1.0. Consideration AUD 9.7 billion; lessee: Future Fund (Australian sovereign wealth fund) + QIC + GIP + OMERS (Canadian pension fund) — an institutional-investor consortium, a pure example of the CFP's "financialization" theme. Additional lock-in element: statutory annual port licence fee ~AUD 80M (CPI-indexed). (Codebook Branch A precedent: "Melbourne: 47 years → 1".)
**Outside the window but critical for Ş2:** the ESC's first 5-yearly review (2016–2021) found "significant and sustained non-compliance" — revenue requirement overstated by AUD 300–650M. A financialized landlord in conflict with its regulator → Ş2 robustness coding and Discussion material.

**(c) Social-legitimacy gap (window 2015–2019) → 0.33**
One documented dispute cluster: the **December 2017 VICT/Webb Dock blockade** — MUA/CFMEU picket continuing despite Supreme Court of Victoria injunctions (the injunction also covered the VTHC and CFMEU secretaries), 1,000+ containers blocked, the port's third stevedore effectively shut down, ~2 weeks. Cluster rule: a single dispute (reinstatement/security card) → 1 cluster = 0.33. Operationally effective (K12: blockade halting throughput) → counts.

**C4 composite:** (0 + 1.0 + 0.33)/3 = **0.44** (previously 0.55 on the pilot sheet).
**Analytical note:** Melbourne does not fall in Durban's "medium-high" band, and the composition is the OPPOSITE: Durban is locked in all three dimensions; Melbourne's lock-in is now ALMOST PURELY financial — the regulatory dimension is mobile, the financial dimension at maximum. "A port that can reform but is leased for 50 years." The sub-dimension decomposition of the composite index is kept on the coding sheet — fsQCA uses the composite, the Discussion tells the decomposition.
